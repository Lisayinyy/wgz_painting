# Style Routes

Three verified Wu Guanzhong vocabularies. Use one verbatim as the `image_synthesize`
prompt. All three were validated against real Wu Guanzhong works.

Adjust only the aspect ratio to match the source orientation. Do not "improve" the
wording — each clause is load-bearing and was added to fix an observed failure.

---

## Route A — Jiangnan water town (江南水乡路) · DEFAULT

Reference works: 《鲁迅故乡》《闹人春色谁家院》. Most legible, strongest popular read of
"Wu Guanzhong". Best for: gardens, pavilions, white-wall-black-tile architecture,
water towns.

```
Repaint this scene completely as an authentic Wu Guanzhong (吴冠中) Chinese ink-and-color painting on raw xuan rice paper. NOT a photograph, NOT a filter, NOT a photo with painterly texture — a real brush painting with visible brush strokes and ink bleed into paper fibre.

Wu Guanzhong's Jiangnan water-town vocabulary, follow strictly:
- PAPER: warm raw xuan paper white, large empty areas left completely blank. At least 45% of the canvas is untouched paper. Emptiness (留白) is the dominant visual element.
- ROOF TILES: the pavilion roofs become bold, saturated pure-black ink masses — wide flat sweeping horizontal brush strokes, edges slightly ragged where wet ink met dry paper. Do NOT draw individual tiles. Each roof is ONE decisive black gesture with a long upward-flicked tail at the eave corner.
- WHITE WALLS: pure blank paper, no shading, no texture, defined only by a thin grey ink line at the base and the black roof above.
- LINE: thin, fluid, uneven-width running ink lines (墨线) for the railing lattice, the pillars, the willow branches. Lines vary in pressure, sometimes break and resume. Willow fronds are long calligraphic downward whipping strokes, not leaves.
- WATER: pale grey wash, a few horizontal dry-brush drags for ripples, mostly blank paper. Vertical grey smudged reflections directly under the black roofs.
- COLOUR: severely restricted. Ink black / grey / paper white carry the whole image. Only these accents allowed: a few pure vermilion-red dots and dashes for any human figure (a figure becomes a small pure red mark, no facial features, no clothing detail), scattered small strokes of clean unmixed sap-green and yellow-green for the foliage masses, one or two tiny ochre dots. Colours are unmixed, clear, applied as flat dots and short dashes floating on the paper — like Wu Guanzhong's 彩点.
- FOLIAGE: NOT rendered leaves. Loose clusters of green dots and quick comma strokes, some running wet into each other, leaving paper showing between them.
- ROCKS: simple grey outline contour with a dry-brush grey wash inside, a few dark ink accents.
- PLAQUES AND COUPLETS: any horizontal name-board above the entrance and any vertical couplet boards on the pillars are rendered as EMPTY blank rectangles — a plain ink outline enclosing untouched paper. Leave them completely wordless. Do NOT copy, imitate, invent or approximate any Chinese characters on them. Any inscription in the source photograph must be dropped, not transcribed.
- Overall: bright, airy, lyrical, high-key. Flat picture plane, no photographic perspective depth, no cast shadows, no light source modelling. Composition simplified to the rhythm of black masses / grey blocks / thin lines / white voids.

Absolutely no writing anywhere in the image: no Chinese characters, no calligraphy, no pseudo-characters or character-like scribbles, no text, no seal, no signature, no border, no frame.
```

## Route B — Shizilin (狮子林路)

Reference work: 《狮子林》. Line-skeleton abstraction that still carries weight. Best
for: rockery, dense foliage, complex textured scenes.

Critical ratio: **black ink dots must outnumber colour dots roughly 4:1**, and the
large pale grey planes must be substantial. Without both, the image goes weightless
and decorative.

```
Repaint this garden scene as an authentic Wu Guanzhong ink painting in the language of his masterwork Shizilin (狮子林). A real brush-and-ink work on raw xuan paper, not a photo, not a filter, not vector art.

The Shizilin structure, follow exactly:
- SKELETON OF WANDERING LINE: the whole surface is organised by long, continuous, meandering ink contour lines of visibly uneven width, enclosing large irregular lobed shapes like eroded garden rockery. Lines swell and taper, occasionally break and resume. They derive from the rocks, railing lattice and roof curves but have become autonomous rhythm.
- LARGE PALE GREY-WHITE PLANES: the shapes enclosed by those lines are filled with broad flat pale grey ink wash and left-blank paper, in an alternating patchwork. These grey planes are substantial and cover much of the surface — they give the picture weight. Flat washes only, no gradient modelling, no volume, no shadow.
- DENSE INK DOTS: scatter numerous ink-black dots and blots of widely varying size and wetness across the grey and white planes. They cluster densely in some regions, thin to nothing in others. Some are fat wet blots that bled into the paper, some tiny dry flecks. These black dots carry the picture's rhythm and are far more numerous than the colour dots.
- FEW COLOUR ACCENTS: only sparse, scattered small colour marks — clean unmixed vermilion, sap green, a little yellow, a little pink. They are clearly a minority, roughly one colour mark for every four or five black dots. Each is a soft irregular brush mark, oval or comma or teardrop with feathered edges, never a perfect circle, never a hard-edged sticker.
- EXTREME BLACK ACCENTS: a few passages of concentrated pure black — the pavilion roof as one wide decisive sweeping stroke, plus two or three narrow solid black vertical bars standing for dark doorways or window voids. Black is deployed as narrow intense marks against white, not as large areas.
- ONE RED: a single tiny vermilion mark as any human presence.
- PLAQUES AND COUPLETS: any name-board or couplet board is an EMPTY blank rectangle — plain ink outline enclosing untouched paper, completely wordless. Do NOT copy, imitate or invent any Chinese characters. Any inscription in the source photograph must be dropped, not transcribed.
- Warm raw xuan paper ground. Flat picture plane, no perspective, no cast shadow, no light source.
- Feeling: dense yet airy, musical, monumental, hovering between recognisable garden and pure abstraction.

Absolutely no writing anywhere in the image: no Chinese characters, no calligraphy, no pseudo-characters or character-like scribbles, no text, no seal, no signature, no border, no frame.
```

## Route C — Shuangyan (双燕路)

Reference work: 《双燕》. The most severe and closest to the real thing. Best for:
white-wall architecture with water reflections, wide flat compositions, minimal scenes.

Critical: the roof ridge band must be **slender** — about one twelfth of canvas height.
An earlier attempt made it thick and the image immediately read as muddy. The explicit
fraction plus "NOT a heavy block" clause is what fixes it.

```
Repaint this garden scene as an authentic Wu Guanzhong ink painting in the language of his masterwork Shuangyan. A real brush-and-ink work on raw xuan paper, not a photo, not a filter.

Structure, follow exactly:
- EXTREME BLACK-AND-WHITE CONTRAST with almost no middle tone.
- THE ROOF RIDGE IS A LONG, SLENDER HORIZONTAL BLACK BAND — emphatically THIN relative to the picture, occupying only a narrow sliver of the canvas height, roughly one twelfth. It is drawn as a single decisive horizontal sweeping stroke, dense black, with a slightly ragged dry lower edge and a small upward flick at each end. It must read as an elegant taut line across the composition, NOT as a heavy block, NOT as a thick bar. Do not let it dominate the upper picture.
- ABOVE THE RIDGE: generous empty blank paper sky. This void above is important and must be clearly larger than the black ridge itself.
- DARK OPENINGS: a few solid pure-black vertical bars, tall and narrow, standing for doorways and window voids against blank white wall. Narrow and intense.
- THE WHITE IS ABSOLUTE: walls are completely blank raw paper, no shading, no texture, no tone. Bounded above by the thin black ridge, below by a soft grey waterline. These blank areas dominate.
- PALE GREY WATER in the lower portion, with soft vertical grey smudges hanging beneath the structures as reflections. Wet, soft, flat, no ripple detail.
- THIN CALLIGRAPHIC LINE: sparse thin ink lines of uneven width for willow fronds as long downward whipping strokes, and the railing suggested with the fewest possible strokes.
- MINIMAL COLOUR: small clusters of clean unmixed sap-green and yellow-green dabs on the foliage, one or two ochre-orange dashes, one single tiny pure vermilion dot as any human presence. Nothing more. Each colour mark is a soft irregular brush dab with feathered edges, never a hard circle.
- PLAQUES AND COUPLETS: any name-board or couplet board is an EMPTY blank rectangle — plain ink outline enclosing untouched paper, completely wordless. Do NOT copy, imitate or invent any Chinese characters. Any inscription in the source photograph must be dropped, not transcribed.
- Warm raw xuan paper ground. Flat picture plane, no perspective depth, no cast shadow, no light modelling.
- Feeling: severe, spare, elegant, silent. Emptiness carries the emotion.

Absolutely no writing anywhere in the image: no Chinese characters, no calligraphy, no pseudo-characters or character-like scribbles, no text, no seal, no signature, no border, no frame.
```

## Subject adaptations

Swap the noun, keep every structural clause:

- **Distant mountains**: "distant mountain ranges become flat pale grey wash silhouettes,
  one or two overlapping layers, no texture, no ridgeline detail, separated from the sky
  by nothing but a soft wet edge."
- **Broad water surface**: "the water is mostly blank paper with a few long horizontal
  dry-brush drags; reflections hang directly beneath their objects as soft vertical grey
  smudges, never mirrored detail."
- **Mist / spray**: "mist is untouched blank paper, defined only by what surrounds it —
  never painted white pigment, never airbrushed gradient."
- **Crowds / figures**: "each person is one small pure vermilion or ink dash, no limbs,
  no faces; a group is a rhythmic scatter of such marks."
